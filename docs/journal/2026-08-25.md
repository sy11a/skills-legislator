# 2026-08-25

## BL-062 — the eval suite stops awarding points for artifacts that do not exist

First case of the v22 cycle, and deliberately not part of v22. The agreed order
recorded at the close of v21 puts BL-060's designs D1+D2 ahead of any law
change, on the argument that fixing law against a ruler that overstates itself
is building on an unverified instrument. Nothing under `skill/` was touched;
VERSION stays 21; no benchmark was owed.

**What the day added to BL-060's finding.** BL-060 blanked two reports. This
case blanked all seven scenarios that read one, and the new data point is
sharper than the original: `legacy-migration-agents-first` scored a **perfect
22/22 and exited 0** with a report containing nothing. BL-060's headline was
audit at 32%; a partial score at least looks like a partial score. A full green
does not look like anything at all.

**The design was already settled**, so the tier was 1 rather than 2 — BL-060
holds the research and `POLICY.md` §1b holds the contract. The work was making
the contract execute: every assert declares the artifact it reads, as a
*required* argument to `Grader.check`, which is the whole enforcement mechanism
and costs one parameter. An artifact that is absent, empty or unparseable makes
its asserts `unmeasured` — a third verdict, fatal to the scenario. One assert
per artifact may go red on its absence: the probe, whose subject *is* the
artifact.

**Two silent skips came out with it**, and they were the more interesting
finds, because neither is visible in a pass rate. `upgrade-drop-stack` guarded
a content assert behind `if has_report:`, and `case-practice` returned early
when no case directory existed — so with the artifact gone the *denominator*
shrank and the suite reported a higher proportion for having measured less.
Deleting the report took that scenario from 15/16 to 14/15 and nothing said so.

**Verification cost nothing**, which is the argument for taking grader cases
first. The v21 workspaces survive on disk, so the whole case is a replay: ten
scenarios reproducing their recorded verdicts (the regression contract), then
the same ten with one report truncated (the red). No agent, no tokens, seconds
per scenario.

**One dead end worth recording.** The obvious replay substrate — the final v21f
workspace — is wrong for `restructure`: its report was overwritten by the
idempotency run 2, which had no `[heal]` item, so replaying it judges a healed
tree against restructure's own never-touch column and reports a violation that
never happened. It read as a real regression in the new grader until v21e
turned out to hold the report the recorded 38/38 was actually taken on. The lesson is general
and belongs to D3: **a scenario's `outputs/` is not immutable after grading**,
so any replay substrate must be validated against its recorded verdict before
it is trusted. The mutation manifest will run on recorded artifacts by design,
so this is the first thing that will bite it.

**Riders.** `POLICY.md` §5 gained the rule the case forced: when the grader
changes and the law does not, the baseline comes from the last commit carrying
the previous law with the *current* grader, never from the previous tag, whose
grader is a different instrument. The v22 baseline is therefore taken from
master at this change, not from `v21`. Separately, `check_static.py` caught a
fleet repository name that had leaked into BL-061's backlog entry, merged the
same day in PR #16; replaced with its alias.

**Open for the next session.** D3 (the mutation manifest) and D4 (pruning) stay
unfiled — D1 proves an artifact was read, not that a present artifact is
measured meaningfully, and no assert may be named for deletion until the
mutation pass can name it by measurement. The substance half of D5 stays open
too: "empty" here means whitespace-only, so a structurally junk report is still
`measured`. And D3 will have to decide what to do about law-branch
conditionals, which make a scenario's denominator vary between runs for reasons
that are entirely lawful.
