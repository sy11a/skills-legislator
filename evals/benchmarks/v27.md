### What the run found, and what it says about the instrument

**Eight reds, and not one of them was the law being wrong.** Every one was the measurement, the
fixture, or the model — which is the finding worth carrying: the corpus above passed at 211/214
in September while three of these defects were already in the tree, latent because no run had
taken the branch that touches them.

- **Three were a grader reading a law's obligation and not the condition the law puts on it.**
  `docs/journal/<today>.md` is owed by a fresh scaffold alone and withheld from an upgrade, and
  the grader demanded it of every mode; `per R-NNN` traceability is owed by **tier 2** and the
  grader demanded it of a case that lawfully declared tier 1; and the target `<today>` was never
  resolved at all, so the assertion could report only failure in *every* mode, including the one
  that owes the file. Each was latent while the runs happened to pick the other branch.
- **One was the product** — `#53`: `FragmentLint` read the fragment home's own scaffolded README
  as a malformed fragment, so a repository went red the moment it adopted the mechanism
  correctly. Found here because `case-practice` asserts the **delivered** engine.
- **One was the fixture** — `evals/fixtures/upgrade-base/CHANGELOG.md` carried `## Unreleased`
  where both Keep a Changelog and `core/changelog.md` write `## [Unreleased]`, so the fixture
  could not satisfy the law it is graded against. POLICY §4's harness class.
- **Two were the model, and the re-run proved it rather than the argument.** `restructure` left
  `{{PROJECT_OVERVIEW}}` unfilled, routing it to `## For the team:` with a defensible reason; the
  second pass filled it, 38/38. That is what a model-class red looks like when it is classified
  honestly: by running it again, not by reasoning about it.

### Methodology, stated because it is not the usual shape

The corpus scenarios were **run** on `dbce8d9` and **graded** on `5ece73b`; the later commits
touch `.github/workflows/dotnet.yml`, `README.md`, an ADR, `tools/publish-legislator.sh` and
`evals/check_static.py`, and none of `skill/`, `src/`, `evals/grade.py`,
`evals/setup_workspace.py` or the fixtures — so no scenario could read a byte of them. Stage 1
was re-run on the final tree (814 .NET tests, `check_static` clean) and every scenario re-graded
there, which is why all eight carry one stamp.

**A re-grade must reproduce the runner's environment, not just its arm.** `arm-integrity`
resolves the arm from `PATH`, not from `PARITY_ENGINE_CMD`, so a hand re-grade in a shell whose
`PATH` holds the *installed* binary grades the audit scenario against a different edition and
reports a red that is the reader's, not the run's. The runner prepends the arm's directory to
`PATH` (`tools/evals-bg.sh:64`) and exports `PARITY_ENGINE_CMD` **and** `PARITY_HOOK_CMD`; all
three are required. This cost an hour here and is written down so it costs nobody else one.

**One standing red is named rather than silenced.** `selftest:derivation` is a stage-1 gate from
this day's work — it held real controls and ran nowhere the benchmark ran, which is how
`audit_slugs_derived` stayed red for three days unremarked. That one failure (`#64`: 22
severity-carrying audit checks, 21 pinned slugs) is the single exception the gate tolerates by
name, and the exception line dies with the issue.

## Harness note: hand-run `grade.py` overwrites the recorded verdict

`evals/grade.py <ws> <scenario>` (the CLI form) writes `grading.json`
**unconditionally on every invocation**, including from a checkout other than
the one that produced the run. Grading `legacy-migration`/`restructure` by
hand from the `origin/master` worktree for the comparison above briefly
overwrote both scenarios' recorded `grading.json` with master-vintage
verdicts; caught before `evals/mutate.py`'s substrate-validation step (which
compares the recorded file against a fresh in-process re-grade) flagged
`legacy-migration` `UNUSABLE`, and repaired by re-grading once more from this
branch. The mutation pass afterward is the one that counts (214/214 killed,
0 unusable) and reflects the corrected, branch-consistent files.
